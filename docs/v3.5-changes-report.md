## 2. Root causes of the owner's lighting complaints (found in source, not assumed)

1. **"Stars and the Milky Way disappear near planets / the background goes empty."** One exposure for everything: the
   Milky Way gain was `LS × 4e-6` and star gain `exposure × 1e-10` with `LS` = the scene exposure. Exposed for a sunlit
   planet (E ≈ 2) the Galactic band rendered at ≈10⁻⁷ display — physically what a short-exposure camera records (NASA,
   "Where are the stars?") but never explained or offered differently. Zooming in/out moved the metering subject in and
   out of frame, so the sky popped in and out ("unpleasant change in brightness").
2. **The Milky Way calibration was arbitrary and double-counted stars.** `4e-6 × map` is ≈100× the measured surface
   brightness of the Galactic band, and the Gaia DR2 map contains every bright star as a dot that the Hipparcos–Tycho and
   GCNS point layers also draw.
3. **Latent depth defect (newly found):** far-plane sprites (stars, constellation lines) were placed at reversed depth
   `1e-9·w`. With the 1e-6 km near plane, a body 20,000 km away has reversed depth ≈ 5·10⁻¹¹ < 10⁻⁹, so **stars passed the
   depth test and were drawn on top of night-side discs**. It was invisible in v3.4 only because the stars themselves were
   exposed away; the first v3.5 capture with a visible sky showed stars across Earth's night side.
4. **Black backsides.** The surface shader had exactly one light (the Sun) and no secondary term: no earthshine, no
   Saturnshine, no inspection option. The auto-exposure also opened without bound on a night side (lit fraction → 0),
   which is what makes night views "pump".
5. **Clipping (EXP-002).** The meter used a reference-grey subject with a blanket −0.45 EV under Neutral; nothing knew
   how bright the brightest parts of a map are. Bright yellow/white maps (Io, Triton, Europa…) were metered like grey
   rock and their highlight channels exceeded the tone curve's shoulder.
6. **Granulation "tiling" (Step 6).** The granule Worley field hashed cells with `fract(p·0.1031)`, a hash that is
   periodic every ≈9.7 lattice cells — visible as regular diagonal bands across the limb close-up.
7. **Additional defects found by the new QA matrix:** the Saturn ring-particle slice stayed visible after the camera left
   Saturn (drawn in front of Earth/Mars in later views); 67P's coma kept its 3000× visibility boost with the camera a
   few km from the nucleus (white frame); the eclipse corona was dimmed by the new night-side cap (fixed before release).

## 3. What v3.5 implements

### 3.1 Viewing modes (new `src/render/presentation.ts`, UI control, setting `viewMode`, key **V**)
Three modes share every astronomical quantity; only the parameters in `PRESENTATION_KEYS` differ, interpolated over
τ = 0.45 s (no popping). Tests assert that the parameter set contains nothing physical and that the solar irradiance
passed to surfaces has no presentation factor.

| Parameter | Scientific | Exploration (default) | Cinematic |
|---|---|---|---|
| sky coupling to scene camera | 1 (physical) | 0.06 | 0.10 |
| star highlight compression (knee/γ) | none | 0.35 / 0.5 | 0.30 / 0.55 |
| diffuse Milky Way × calibrated | 1 | 0.8 | 1.05 |
| night-side inspection light (× local sunlight) | 0 | 0.03 | 0.015 |
| max auto-exposure opening on a night side | unbounded | +1.5 EV | +1.2 EV |
| airglow display gain | 1 | 0.12 | 0.25 |
| contrast / saturation / vignette | 1 / 1 / 0 | 1 / 1 / 0 | 1.08 / 1.06 / 0.16 |
| Sun disc meter weight | v3.4 (0.25) | ×0.8 | ×0.7 |

The active mode is always visible (top-centre control) together with live labels: "Night side: inspection light (not
sunlight)", "Sky: dark-adapted exposure", or "One physical camera".

### 3.2 HDR exposure system
- **Scene meter** (`meterLuminance`): v3.4 reference-grey subject kept for overall brightness; **highlight protection**
  from measured per-map statistics (`tools/ingest/texstats_hi.py` → `texstats_hi.json`: area-weighted 99th percentile of
  max(R,G,B), noData excluded) × the exact photometric peak of the body's law at the current phase
  (`photometricPeak`, mirrors the WGSL Lunar–Lambert/Minnaert) — the brightest 1 % of a resolved disc is placed at 1.25
  linear (Neutral → 0.917, sRGB ≈ 245; tuned from an initial 0.9 that darkened Earth by 1.4 EV). The blanket −0.45 EV is removed, so dark bodies are no longer darkened.
- **Night cap**: the lit fraction may open the meter at most `nightCapEV` beyond full-lit metering (Scientific:
  unbounded). Disabled during an eclipse so the corona is metered as before.
- **Sky exposure** (`skyExposure`): own dark-adapted domain (`SKY_REF_EXPOSURE` = 3·10⁶, the look of a ~20 s f/2.8
  ISO-3200 frame), weak coupling, physical veil (daylit air around the camera, the Sun on screen) re-couples it so stars
  never shine through a daylit sky. User `skyEV` is a clean multiplier.
- Temporal adaptation unchanged (asymmetric log-space), plus smoothed veil; `settleExposure()` also snaps the mode
  parameters for deterministic captures.

### 3.3 Astronomical background
- **Diffuse Milky Way** (`tools/ingest/sky_diffuse.py`): the Gaia map with point sources removed (5×5 median + 3×3 box;
  9.6 % of the map's flux removed), so catalogue stars are not double-counted; **calibrated** to μ_V ≈ 21.0 mag arcsec⁻²
  in the band with a contrast exponent bringing the poles to ≈23.4 and a floor at 23.6 (DERIVED; Leinert et al. 1998);
  ±½-LSB dither. Still labelled a 2D panorama, not the 3D Milky Way.
- **Stars**: far-plane depth fixed (exactly z = 0 in reversed depth) → correct occlusion by every body; PSF highlight
  compression keeps magnitude order while preventing bright stars from ballooning; `starBrightness` default returns to
  the calibrated 1 (v3.4 had a hidden 3×; migrated).
- **Unresolved worlds** join the sky domain (sky exposure + compression), so a distant Jupiter is brighter than the stars
  around it again; the Sun's point source stays on the scene exposure.

### 3.4 Planet illumination
- **Planetshine** (physical): for every resolved body the neighbour delivering the most reflected sunlight (parent or a
  moon > 400 km) is a secondary light, E = (2/3)·A·(R/d)²·Φ(α)/r², with a wrap term for the extended source; Earth's
  clouds included in its received reflectance. Earthshine on the Moon ≈ 5·10⁻⁵ of sunlight (invisible in Scientific at
  normal exposure — correct).
- **Inspection light** (presentation, labelled): a soft fill from the observer's side tilted toward the body's north,
  confined beyond the terminator (faded in over μ₀ 0.06 → −0.22), per-family law (Minnaert for giants), 3 % of local
  sunlight in Exploration, 0 in Scientific.
- **Airglow** (Earth): Gaussian 557.7 nm-dominated layer at 95 km, calibrated to ≈ V 22 mag arcsec⁻² at the zenith, only
  where the layer is in night, in the sky exposure domain.

### 3.5 Sun
- Aperiodic **PCG3D** hash for granule cells + a second warp octave → the diagonal banding is gone in the limb view.
- Exploration/Cinematic expose the resolved disc ≈ +0.3/+0.5 EV (shoulder of the tone curve: luminous centre, warm limb).
- **AUD-07**: v3.4's claim of "visible sunspot groups at 0.05 AU" is withdrawn; the new `sun_spots` metric is part of the
  QA evidence and the docs only describe features the metric supports.

### 3.6 Rendering composition (Three.js r186 / TSL / WebGPU — APIs verified in the installed sources)
One HDR scene pass (half-float, 4× MSAA) → presentation grade (TSL `Fn`, identity except Cinematic) → bloom (threshold
6× display white, × mode) → renderer tone mapping (Neutral / AgX) → sRGB. Ordering: sky (opaque, far) → bodies (opaque)
→ stars/points (additive, depth-tested at the far plane) → atmospheres (premultiplied over) → glare. No extra passes
were added: the ten conceptual layers of spec §10 map onto these with uniforms.
